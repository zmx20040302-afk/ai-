## Abstract

Design-space exploration for piezoelectric micromachined ultrasonic transducers is expensive and
vulnerable to labels that are numerically present but physically incomparable: resonance order can
switch, and a −3 dB bandwidth is undefined when a crossing leaves the scan window.
We present **PeakGuard**, an abstention-aware surrogate flow coupling resonance-semantic labelling,
mixed continuous–discrete modelling, calibrated intervals and fail-closed validity checks, with
evidence reported per corpus, never pooled. Three results are negative: under leakage-free grouped
splits with five seeds and eight methods none beats a persistence baseline on either formal target;
the registered energy-closure gate fails every caliber correction we can test; and a multi-frequency
reduced model misses the 2 % key-region threshold by 10×. The closed loop pays off instead: under a
pre-registered fixed budget it reaches zero simple regret after five evaluations, with 96 % of
un-gated proposals outside the supported domain. PeakGuard is a screening and scheduling layer, not a
solver replacement.

**Index Terms**—PMUT, design-space exploration, surrogate modelling, abstention, first resonance
peak, multi-physics simulation.

## 1. Introduction

A PMUT converts electrical drive into out-of-plane membrane motion and thence into an acoustic
pressure field. Its resonance frequency, displacement, pressure sensitivity, and bandwidth depend
jointly on diaphragm geometry, electrode coverage, layer stack, and array layout. Prior work uses
strain mode shapes to guide electrode design and validates the resulting configurations with full
electro-acoustic finite-element (FE) models [1]; others improve displacement or bandwidth through
mass loading and electrode-coverage studies [2], [3]. The physics is therefore well understood, but
sweeping several continuous and discrete parameters with a full multi-physics model remains
expensive.

Surrogate models and learning-based optimisation are standard in analog design automation: DNN-Opt
reduces the samples needed for circuit sizing [4]; knowledge-infused learning embeds topology and
performance coupling [5]; multi-agent decomposition shrinks the search space [6]; probabilistic
rollouts cut the number of real simulations [7]; recent frameworks interleave several algorithms to
obtain superadditive gains [8]. Two-stage surrogates [9] and comparative surrogate studies [10]
likewise target sample efficiency. The shared objective is not a small average prediction error but
finding feasible, high-quality designs under a fixed simulation budget.

Applying the same recipe to PMUTs exposes three problems that average error hides.

**P1 — Resonance identity.** A frequency response may contain several local peaks, and the global
maximum of one design need not belong to the same mode order as that of another. Treating "the peak"
as a scalar value silently mixes labels of different physical origin.

**P2 — Incomplete and censored labels.** When the first peak approaches the scan boundary, one or
both −3 dB crossings may be missing, so the bandwidth is not a complete label. If the peak itself
sits on an endpoint, the peak frequency is only a lower bound (right-censored). Such combinations
must not enter a ranking as ordinary measurements.

**P3 — Mixed design space and unsupported extrapolation.** Continuous geometry and integer array
parameters form a mixed space. A plain regressor still returns a number outside its training region,
without saying whether any evidence supports it.

This paper contributes:

1. **C1 — a semantically consistent first-peak labelling protocol.** Complete peak, no interior peak,
   unclosed bandwidth, right-censored peak, truncated upper crossing, violated structural
   constraints, and out-of-domain inputs are represented as distinct states, with an explicit
   state-to-field mapping.
2. **C2 — a layered surrogate for mixed continuous–discrete inputs with fail-closed abstention**
   driven by the input domain, the integer structural constraint, an extrapolation-step limit, and
   quarantined combinations.
3. **C3 — an evaluation protocol that names what fails.** We audit the bandwidth caliber, check
   cross-batch consistency, and compare eight methods against persistence under leakage-free grouped
   splits with five seeds, reporting the channels that gain nothing instead of a pooled score.
4. **C4 — physics-consistency gates reported as experimental results, including the negative ones**:
   S3 fails; S4 passes only under its declared scope and object; the strict mesh-convergence caliber
   is not met; the solver state stays `draft`.

The present evidence supports trend screening and scheduling of high-fidelity re-checks. It does not
support replacing the FE solver, and it does not support any engineering absolute value.

## 2. Related Work

**PMUT design and modelling.** Mode-shape-guided electrode design [1], mass-loaded arrays [2], and
radial electrode-coverage studies [3] all rely on full FE simulation per candidate. This is precisely
the cost that motivates surrogates, and also the reason a surrogate's validity domain must be stated:
the underlying response is resonant and therefore strongly non-smooth in the design parameters.

**Surrogates and learning-based sizing.** DNN-Opt [4], knowledge infusion [5], multi-agent
reinforcement learning [6], probabilistic rollouts [7], and interworking frameworks [8] reduce
simulation counts for analog circuits, where the label set is well defined. Two-stage surrogate
modelling [9] and surrogate comparisons for signal integrity [10] address accuracy and cost but
assume complete labels. Our focus is the complementary failure mode: labels that exist numerically
yet are not comparable, and predictions that should be refused rather than returned.

**Validation of surrogates.** In this work the surrogate is not only scored by error; it is placed
behind the same kind of consistency gates used for solvers: limit consistency, mesh behaviour,
conservation, and coverage of calibrated intervals. We report gate outcomes verbatim, including
failures, because a validity-aware flow is only useful if its refusals and its negative results are
visible.

## 3. Problem Definition and Label Semantics

**Design variables.** Geometry is varied by a continuous scale factor *x* applied to the membrane
layout, together with two integer array parameters *N₁* (drive-electrode winding count) and *N₂*
(sense-electrode winding count), subject to the structural constraint *N₁* < *N₂*. Frequency is a
separate axis; the present corpus is measured on a fixed frequency grid per batch.

**Response channels.** Eight channels are stored per combination: `bandwidth_Hz`, `Q_factor`,
`f_peak_Hz`, `peak_SPL_dB`, `abs_w_m` (displacement), `abs_p_Pa` and `abs_p_per_V_Pa` (pressure and
pressure per volt), and `abs_vz_struct_m3s` (structural velocity). The source table reports that in the
table `abs_p_Pa` and `abs_p_per_V_Pa` are **byte-identical in all 2005 rows**, i.e. the nominal
eight channels contain **seven independent** ones. All statistics in this paper are therefore stated
per channel *and* per batch, never pooled, and the duplicate pair is flagged wherever channel counts
appear.

**States.** The first observable interior local maximum inside the scan window defines the
first-order resonance peak. The label states are: (i) **complete peak**, both −3 dB crossings inside
the window; (ii) **no interior peak**; (iii) **unclosed bandwidth** (one crossing missing);
(iv) **right-censored peak** (the maximum lies on the window endpoint, so the reported peak frequency
is a lower bound); (v) **truncated upper crossing**; (vi) **structurally invalid** (*N₁* ≥ *N₂*);
(vii) **out of training domain**. A channel value is *defined* for fitting only when the value exists,
is positive, and its state condition is satisfied; ratios in this paper always report the count of
rows that are defined in this sense, never a flag-based denominator.

**Goal.** Given a corpus of simulated combinations, learn a mapping from (*x*, *N₁*, *N₂*) to the
response channels, quantify its uncertainty, refuse predictions without evidence, and use the result
to allocate a fixed budget of high-fidelity simulations.

## 4. PeakGuard

**4.1 Semantic labelling (C1).** The scan spectrum of each combination is reduced to the state
description of §3 plus the scalar channels, using the exported spectrum and, where available, the
exported peak/bandwidth table. The mapping from each state to the stored fields is tabulated so that
every row can be re-derived and audited; rows whose bandwidth is unclosed or whose peak is censored
are excluded from bandwidth/Q targets rather than imputed.

**4.2 Layered surrogate (C2).** Continuous and discrete inputs are treated separately: within a
fixed integer pair, the continuous axis is modelled with shape-preserving interpolation
(PCHIP) and the integer axis with a per-mode regression, with the two combined in a hierarchical
scheme. Competing global models (linear, kernel ridge, quadratic ridge, Gaussian process, gradient
boosting, MLP) are evaluated as alternatives rather than assumed inferior.

**4.3 Abstention.** A prediction is refused when any of the following holds: the query leaves the
declared applicability box; the integer constraint *N₁* < *N₂* is violated; the requested
extrapolation exceeds the declared number of steps; the combination is on the quarantine list; or the
requested channel is undefined for that state. Refusals are counted and reported; the flow never
substitutes a value silently, and out-of-domain queries are referred back to the FE solver.

**4.4 Calibrated intervals.** Interval predictions are produced on the calibrated subset and are
evaluated by empirical coverage in the range required by the solver specification, with interval
width reported alongside coverage. A narrow interval with low coverage is reported as a failure, not
as precision.

## 5. Experimental Setup

**Model and mesh.** The FE model is a 3-D piezoelectric–acoustic PMUT stack (seven-layer device on a
handle wafer) solved in the frequency domain with a fixed design point per simulation. The mesh used
for all physics-gate numbers below is the pre-registered caliber: a boundary mesh on the
solid/electrode side plus a tetrahedral acoustic mesh of `lamda/5` referenced to
`fmax = 35 MHz`, so that the mesh does **not** change with the solved frequency — a requirement for
any limit or comparison argument.

**Data.** Two scan grids coexist and are never merged: batch C (5–50 MHz, 91 frequencies,
1,782 rows) and batch A (5–35 MHz, 61 frequencies, 223 rows), plus a 250-row frozen batch V2 kept
separate because its flags are proxies rather than measured states. Of the C rows, 1,523 (85.5 %)
have a closed two-sided −3 dB bandwidth and 673 meet the pre-set quality-factor gate; for A the
corresponding numbers are 154 of 223 (69.1 %) and 55.

**Splits.** All learned models use grouped, leakage-free splits in which an entire combination key
(geometry family) belongs either to training or to hold-out; five split seeds are used. The split
assignment is an external artifact whose training/test key sets are disjoint for every seed.

**Baselines and metrics.** Eight methods are compared: persistence (copy the nearest available
neighbour in the integer direction within the same continuous layer), PCHIP, linear regression,
kernel ridge, quadratic ridge, Gaussian process, gradient boosting, and an MLP. The primary metric is
the median relative error `|pred − truth| / truth` with the count of defined rows; the paired
comparison metric is `lift_paired` = median error of persistence minus median error of the method on
the same secondary subset, so that a positive value means genuine added value. No weighted total
score is computed.

**Gate semantics.** Physics gates are taken from the solver specification, whose thresholds are
S1 (key region < 2 %, global < 5 %), S2 (engineering quantities < 3 %), S3 (conservation < 0.5 %,
boundary conditions < 1 %), S4 (mesh difference < 2 %) and S5 (interval coverage in [0.85, 0.95]).
An `inconclusive` outcome is never reported as a pass, and thresholds are never adjusted after the
fact.

## 6. Results

### 6.1 Label validity is part of the design problem

In batch C, 259 of 1,782 rows (14.5 %) do not have a closed two-sided bandwidth, and of the 1,523
closed rows **673 (44.2 %) meet the quality-factor gate**; in batch A, 69 of 223 rows (30.9 %) are
not closed and 55 of the 154 closed rows (35.7 %) meet the gate. Both counts are taken from
the source table's own columns — `bandwidth_closed` for closure and `q_gate_pass` (interval 2.5–3.5)
for the gate, whose applicability column equals the closed subset exactly — **not** from the
downstream `peak_closed` flag, which counts 1,612 and 161 closed rows respectively and would
therefore give a different denominator. These are not noise: they are the expected consequence of a
first peak that drifts towards or across the scan window as the design moves. Any pipeline that
treats them as ordinary targets silently mixes complete, censored, and truncated labels; §4.1
therefore excludes them from the bandwidth and Q targets and reports the surviving counts.

### 6.2 The bandwidth caliber must be named

Two independent audit comparisons exist, and they must not be merged into one sentence.

* The single-degree-of-freedom fitted bandwidth versus batch A's own `from_points` estimate, on the
  same peak: **median |ΔBW|/BW = 20.3 %**, p90 27.2 %, within-10 % rate 11.0 %, Spearman 0.9888
  (n = 91) — a systematic ≈0.8× scale offset with a **rank-robust** ordering.
* The locally extracted bandwidth versus the exported table over the same combinations:
  **median 33.5 %**, p90 88.1 %, within-10 % rate 21.9 % (n = 292), and the quality factors of the
  two calibers correlate only 0.2226 — i.e. this comparison is **not** rank-robust, and the exported
  table is retained as the primary caliber for Q and bandwidth.

Consequently, absolute bandwidth values are reported with their caliber; cross-caliber comparisons
are restricted to rankings, and only where the rank correlation was verified.

### 6.3 Strong baselines: no method beats persistence on the two targets

Under leakage-free grouped splits, per-batch training, and five seeds, 20 channel×batch cells are
decidable. For the two formal targets the outcome is negative and consistent across seeds:

| channel (batch C) | best method | median relative error | `lift_paired` (median) | seeds +/ − |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bandwidth_Hz` | gradient boosting | 0.01436 | **−0.005988** | 0 / 5 |
| `Q_factor` | gradient boosting | 0.009553 | **−0.001968** | 0 / 5 |

The persistence baseline itself has median relative error 0.007647 (`bandwidth_Hz`) and 0.007321
(`Q_factor`) on the same batch. Across the full table, only 10 of 20 cells contain any method that is
positive in all five seeds, and in 8 of 20 cells the best method has a negative median lift. Four
channels do gain value (`peak_SPL_dB`, `abs_w_m`, `abs_p_Pa`/`abs_p_per_V_Pa`, `abs_vz_struct_m3s`),
but these are precisely the channels whose persistence baseline is weak because adjacent integer
layers differ substantially.

These numbers were re-derived by an independent implementation that does not import the main
evaluation code: the artifact manifest (12 files) verifies hash-for-hash, the training/test key sets
are disjoint for all five seeds, and the persistence median 0.007726751204325416 and its
defined-row count (416) reproduce exactly. We report the limits of that verification as
well: the full 8-method table, the six ablations, the PCHIP implementation, batch V2, and the
holdout-label-based active-sampling study were **not** independently recomputed.

The conclusion is deliberately narrow: *in this corpus, under these calibers, exchanging the
surrogate for a stronger regressor does not pay off on the two formal targets.* It does not show that
surrogate modelling is useless — it shows that label semantics, not model capacity, is the binding
constraint.

### 6.4 Physics-consistency gates

**S3 fails.** On the second model carrier — an already-solved higher-resolution output of the same
device lineage — the registered gate gives 7 passes, 2 failures and 0 inconclusive outcomes, so the
gate verdict is *failed* and the release flag for that carrier stays closed. The failures are
`energy_loss_radiation_separation` = **0.0617676 (6.177 %)** against a 0.5 % threshold and
`conservation_current_circuit_vs_field_flux` = **0.111187 (11.12 %)** against 0.5 %. The passes
include closed-surface charge conservation (8.151e-04), an integrated sound-hard weak-form residual
(1.996e-04), both potential boundary conditions (3.3e-16, 0.0), and two symmetry checks by exact
first-moment zero tests (max 3.326e-04 and 7.449e-05). One pass is structurally non-independent: the
circuit terminal and the physics terminal share the same ODE unknown.

**S4 passes only under its declared scope and object.** On the main sensitivity axis (the acoustic
bulk mesh `ftet1`, nine levels, 72 ordered pairs, same batch and same machine) the **key region**
(`R ≤ R_max/3`, 51 of 1369 probe points) is **0.6753 % < 2 %**; the full-domain value 1.5504 % is
reported for reference only. Richardson extrapolations under three caliper choices give 1.3446 %,
1.4300 %, and 1.4963 % — all below 2 %, but all are extrapolations rather than measurements. Three
limits travel with this verdict: it covers only pairwise differences **between the levels tested**;
it must **not** be described as mesh independence; and the older ladder quoted in an earlier ruling
(key region 1.5191 %, full domain 2.4161 %) belongs to a different level set and must be reported
separately.

**Strict mesh convergence of the device is not established.** The recorded strict-caliber L1 check
judges `passed = false`: on the same three-level ladder, the target quantities (`Ptot`, identical to
the first global scalar `gev1`) differ by up to **2.235 %** between the coarser pair against a 1 %
criterion, while the finer pair is already inside 1 %; the next finer level (×0.5, 342,635 elements)
failed on local memory at 18.55 GB physical / 19.59 GB virtual, and an attempt to fit it by
assembling a multi-block segregated solver was measured to be infeasible through the Java API
available here. A later four-level run on a 96-core server did solve the ×0.5 level (peak 54.08 GB
physical) and reports a monotone ladder with every adjacent difference inside 1 % for the finest
pair; the pre-registered **strict** rule (every adjacent pair below 1 %) nevertheless still fails,
because the coarsest pair lies outside the asymptotic range. The human reviewer subsequently approved,
on 2026-10-10, a **finest-pair** caliber (the finest adjacent pair below 1 %); under that approved
caliber the four-level ladder passes, while the original strict record is retained unchanged as
history. Both readings are therefore reported, and neither is presented as grid independence.
Consequently no grid-convergence claim is made, and the library's
single passing L1 record is a four-element heat-conduction toy model whose own caveat states that it
cannot substitute for a non-linear multi-physics convergence study.

**Two different objects must not be confused: local x-family models versus the field-level gate.**
On five held-out continuous values of the declared x-family (20 MHz), a shape-preserving local model
("model B") meets all three thresholds — key-region relative L2 **0.69999 %** with full-domain
1.09097 % for *N₁* = 4 and 0.95735 % / 1.13266 % for *N₁* = 7, and an S2 engineering maximum of
**0.739 %** — while a raw-POD variant ("model A") fails the key region at 2.126 %. Four caveats must
travel with that statement: the independence of the two routes is self-declared by the script rather
than enforced; the solver spec's `model_id` field is empty; the local model class has no save/load
API, so "reproduction" currently means re-running a script rather than loading a frozen model; and
and the coverage caliber itself was re-adjudicated by the human reviewer on 2026-10-10: the
previously supplemental **8-fold protocol**, which yields empirical coverage 0.9259 and 0.9383 with
interval widths 1.30–1.83 % and 1.78–3.64 % respectively, was **promoted to the official coverage
caliber** (target coverage band [0.85, 0.95] at α = 0.10). The earlier five-point protocol remains
recorded as *inconclusive* and is not deleted; the two calibers must not be mixed.

**Field-level thresholds.** After correcting an object mismatch (a 2 % criterion had been applied to
the full domain), the field-level gate over the frozen snapshot set is **0/7** folds at the
key-region 2 % object (maximum 0.858054, model `norm_shape_pchip`; 51 of 1369 points), **0/7** at the
S2 3 % engineering object (maximum 0.761833, authoritative `best_model` = `raw_linear`), and **2/7**
at the global 5 % object (maximum 0.808890). Reporting discipline matters here: on the S2 object two
models have *bit-identical* maxima, and the fold-pass count differs between them (`raw_linear` 0/7
versus `raw_pchip` 3/7); the authoritative artifact names `raw_linear`, and attributing 3/7 to "the
best model" would be a silent model swap that flatters the result. The gate's conclusion is unchanged
— the release flag stays closed, and a group of **405 combinations must not be expanded**; moreover that
pipeline lies outside the declared applicability scope.

### 6.5 Diagnosing the energy-closure residual

Because S3's energy item is the only failure that could plausibly be a caliber defect, we probed it
directly (all probes are zero-solve post-processing on already-solved models).

| candidate cause | measurement | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| port-voltage convention (floating terminal treated as 0 V) | electrode mean potentials: drive −1.000000 V, ground 0, floating terminal ≤ 7.7 µV; including it changes `P_in` by +0.053 %, i.e. 6.9e-06 of the apparent power | **refuted** |
| current post-expression omits the piezoelectric coupling | `∫es.JZ dA ≡ jω·∫es.Dz dA` on all four electrode sets (agreement to all printed digits) | **refuted** |
| acoustic power uses a plane-wave approximation | the model's `Ptot` equals the exact flux `Im(K)/(2ωρ)`, `K = ∫ p·conj(∇p·n) dA`, at all six frequencies (agreement to all printed digits) | **refuted** |
| integration surface chosen badly | two disjoint surfaces agree to 1.8 % at 20 MHz, but **both exceed** the electrical input: 3.996822e-06 W (outer hemisphere) and 3.924776e-06 W (fluid–structure interface) versus 3.734359e-06 W | **refuted** |
| the sound-hard residual holds only for a single test function | extended to φ ∈ {1, x, y}: residuals ≤ 0.045 % (φ = z is degenerate — the surface is the z = 0 symmetry plane) | **passes** |
| the residual is unconverged discretisation | under the refinement we can afford, `P_asi` changes by 1e-06 relative and `P_in` by 1.4e-05; the solid-side mechanical power independently reproduces the acoustic side to 0.02 %; the gap stays at **−4.39 %** | **not confirmed** |
| the residual is a fixed systematic offset | same caliber on a second geometry at the same 20 MHz: +1.60 % (coupling surface) and −0.34 % (outer hemisphere) instead of −4.12 % / −5.82 %; within one geometry, 5–15 MHz gives +3.14…+3.97 % and 25–35 MHz gives −1.52…−0.82 % (coupling surface) | **refuted** |

The calibrated statement is therefore: **at this design point** the *electrical* input power is
about 4.4 % below the acoustic flux measured on two mutually independent surfaces, and this is a
genuine discrete non-closure rather than a post-processing mistake.

**The residual is design-point- and frequency-dependent, and must be quoted per point.** Two further
zero-solve measurements were made with the *same* caliber. First, the identical post-processing applied
to a second, independently meshed carrier of the *same* design point reproduces the shortfall: input
power 3.7338 µW against 3.8943 µW (coupling surface) and 3.9645 µW (outer hemisphere), i.e. −4.12 %
and −5.82 %, versus −4.85 % and −6.57 % on the first carrier, with the three powers agreeing between
carriers to within 0.81 %. The direction of the residual, however, is *not* universal: at the same
20 MHz on a second geometry the same caliber yields **+1.60 %** against the coupling surface and
**−0.34 %** against the outer hemisphere — the latter comfortably inside the 0.5 % criterion — and
within a single geometry the sign flips across the band (5–15 MHz: +3.14…+3.97 %; 25–35 MHz:
−1.52…−0.82 % against the coupling surface, −4.08…−5.34 % against the outer hemisphere). A single
figure such as "≈4.4 %" is therefore valid for the reported design point only and must never be
presented as a property of the method.

Three caliber limits must be quoted with it: (i) energy statements whose numerator is `P_in` are only
meaningful above ≈2 MHz, because at lower frequencies the real part of the terminal current is at the
level of the linear solver's resolution (5e-08 of the apparent power); (ii) post-processing normals
(`nX/nY/nZ`) must not be used for closed-surface flux judges — on the 28-face electrode surface they
give `∫nz dA = −6.65e-09 ≠ 0` and a spurious non-conservation ratio of 2.16, whereas the physics
normal `es.nx/ny/nz` gives 1.03e-03, inside the 0.5 % criterion (reproduced independently on the
second carrier: 8.2e-04 versus 0.999); (iii) because the sign of the residual depends on the
integration surface at some frequencies, the surface caliber must be named in every such statement.
The refinement that would settle the question (halving the solid-side mesh) failed on local memory at
900,182 degrees of freedom with a peak physical memory of 19.14 GB; this is recorded as an
infrastructure limit, not as divergence.

### 6.6 Closed-loop design-space exploration

**Closed-loop design-space exploration (pre-registered, completed).** A fixed-budget comparison
was pre-registered before any of its solves: a pool of 1080 candidate designs, five shared initial
points, and four arms — random, greedy (best-so-far local neighbour), expected-improvement with a
Gaussian process, and the same with an applicability gate — totalling 105 high-fidelity evaluations
of a **single-frequency** objective (radiated acoustic power per volt² at 20 MHz, computed with the
caliber validated in §6.5). The objective spans **574×** across the 93 designs actually evaluated,
so it is discriminative, and the campaign finished in 123 minutes with a **2 % failure rate** (below
the 20 % pre-registered inconclusive threshold).

| arm | successful calls | best objective (W/V²) | simple regret | best at k = 5 | top-10 % hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random (5 seeds × 5) | 25 | 2.1756e-05 | 18.8 % | 8.409e-06 (median) | 0.0 % |
| greedy | 25 | 2.2807e-05 | 14.9 % | 4.863e-06 | 4.0 % |
| GP + expected improvement | 25 | **2.6786e-05** | **0.00 %** | **2.640e-05** | **24.0 %** |
| GP + EI **with applicability gate** | 23 | 2.6126e-05 | 2.46 % | 2.510e-05 | 17.4 % |

The pre-registered primary endpoint is decisive in this budget: after **five** evaluations the
GP-based arms reach 2.640e-05 and 2.510e-05, i.e. they exceed **every one of the five random seeds**
(quantile 1.00) — and both exceed the random arm's best value over its **entire 25-call** budget
(2.1756e-05). The gated arm was 2.46 % short of the campaign optimum while proposing points with
training support: a post-hoc replay of its 25 steps shows that **24 of the 25 ungated
expected-improvement proposals would have landed outside the corpus-covered domain** (96 %), so the
gate is load-bearing rather than cosmetic (this replay reuses the same proposal code path and is a
diagnostic, not an independent verification). An independently written script re-derived all of the
figures above from the append-only ledger: **34 checks, 0 mismatches** at 1e-12, nine repeated
design evaluations bit-identical (deterministic cache), and the secondary-surface caliber agreeing to
**1.6–2.1 %** across the campaign, consistent with the surface-independence measured in §6.5.

The claim is bounded: the oracle is **single-frequency**, so this is a protocol-efficiency
comparison and **not** a bandwidth-optimisation result; only the random arm has seed replication, so
the GP findings are quantile statements rather than significance tests; and the objective's own mesh
sensitivity (≤1.3 %, §6.5) restricts the numbers to relative comparisons.

**Multi-frequency ROM (pre-registered; completed on a 96-core server).** The third experiment asks
whether the single-frequency evidence extends along the frequency axis. The protocol was registered
before its solves: two local families (*N₁* = 4 and 7, *N₂* = 8), twelve training and three held-out
continuous values per family, thirteen frequencies per design point (four far from the pilot peak,
six near it, and three **reserved** frequencies never used in fitting), 406 solves in total, with
acceptance defined only on *unseen-x × unseen-frequency* cross cells. Because the local machine could
not commit the ~16 GB that one solve requires (free commit ≈ 14.5 GB), the batch ran on a 96-core
server with an identical model, mesh and frequency set; the execution change is registered per point
(host, COMSOL version, thread count). 24 main and 6 held-out design points solved with **zero
failures**.

*Pipeline regression target.* Before any acceptance claim, the new pipeline was compared with the
pre-existing exported snapshots at 20 MHz on the same frozen 1369-point set: **global relative L2
1.23 % / 1.30 %** and **key-region 0.50 % / 0.56 %** for the two families (maximum |p| agreeing to
0.04 % / 0.6 %). Two independently produced pipelines therefore agree at the percent level, so a
subsequent ROM failure cannot be blamed on data acquisition.

*Result: S1 fails, and the failure is diagnosed rather than merely reported.* On the cross cells the
key-region relative L2 reaches **27.1 %** (*N₁* = 4) and **30.9 %** (*N₁* = 7) against a 2 %
threshold, with global values 26.6 % / 30.6 % against 5 %. A sensitivity study then separates the two
candidate causes: raising the reduced-basis order from the registered k = 4 to the full rank k = 120
drives the training reconstruction error to **0.000 %** while the cross-cell error is essentially
unchanged (18.52 % / 27.61 %). The reduced basis is therefore *not* the limiting factor; the error
comes from interpolating the coefficients over the (*x*, *f*) grid. A post-hoc axis decomposition at
k = 16 attributes it to both axes, with the frequency axis dominating for one family (19.9 % versus
14.6 % for *N₁* = 4) and the geometry axis for the other (22.4 % versus 3.6 % for *N₁* = 7). The
actionable conclusion is that the sampling density and the interpolation scheme — not the basis size —
must change before a frequency-axis ROM can be certified; S5 (interval coverage) is deliberately left
unevaluated because its (*x*, *f*) protocol has not been pre-registered.

## 7. Discussion

**Semantics before capacity.** The negative baseline result of §6.3 and the label analysis of §6.1
point the same way: in this problem the binding constraint is whether a number is a comparable
measurement, not how expressive the regressor is. Spending capacity on a target whose label is
censored in 15–31 % of rows buys nothing.

**Abstention is a feature with a price.** Refusing out-of-domain queries prevents silent
extrapolation, but it also removes coverage: in the ablation, disabling abstention raises coverage to
1 while the fallback-filled subset frequently has *lower* median error than the abstaining arm,
i.e. "abstention blocks the hard cases, therefore it is more accurate" does not follow. Any gate must
be reported together with the coverage it costs.

**Gates are results.** Three of the four physics checks reported here are negative or
narrowly-scoped. Reporting them as results rather than as internal diagnostics is what makes the
refusal mechanism auditable — and it is also what forbids the engineering claims that a
smaller-error surrogate would tempt one to make.

## 8. Conclusion

PeakGuard couples resonance-semantic labelling, a layered mixed-input surrogate, calibrated
intervals, and fail-closed abstention for PMUT design-space exploration. On the present corpus its
honest summary is: label validity and gate status, not prediction accuracy, dominate usefulness; no
tested method beats persistence on the two formal targets; the physics-consistency gate fails on
energy closure, and that failure survives every caliber correction we could test. The flow is
therefore offered as a candidate screener and high-fidelity scheduling layer. Experimental
anchoring, a validated field-level solver, and a frequency-axis reduced model that meets the
key-region criterion remain future work; the fixed-budget closed loop and the first multi-frequency
protocol are reported here, the latter as a diagnosed failure.

**Limitations.** (i) No experimental data and no literature evidence rows are in the repository;
all statements are simulation-internal. (ii) Strict mesh convergence is not established, so no
engineering absolute value may be quoted. (iii) The model's own status is `draft`; no state has been
promoted. (iv) A large re-export of a second scan grid (648 combinations × 91 frequencies) is
available, but its overlap regression against the earlier grid fails (maximum |Δ| = 0.08849 dB over
4,392 overlapping points) because the two exports differ in source model and scan definition; it is
reported as a limitation, not as a pipeline failure. (v) Both pre-registered experiments of §6.6 are complete; their
narrow or negative outcomes are reported as results, not withheld. (vi) The energy-closure residual of
§6.5 is a *per-point* quantity that changes sign with frequency and design point; the value quoted for
the reported design point is not a global constant, and the supporting measurements were obtained by
zero-solve post-processing of already-solved models rather than by new solves.
