## Abstract

How should a surrogate model of a resonant multiphysics design task be evaluated? We answer with a
frozen, released benchmark built on a piezoelectric micromachined ultrasonic transducer (PMUT)
parameter sweep, in which labels are numerically present but often physically incomparable: the
resonance order can switch, and a −3 dB bandwidth is undefined once a crossing leaves the scan
window. **PeakGuard-Bench** couples resonance-semantic labelling, mixed continuous–discrete
modelling, calibrated intervals and fail-closed abstention into seven specified evaluation tasks,
and reports every result per corpus, never pooled. Two results are negative and one is scoped: under duplicate-aware grouped
splits with five seeds and eight methods none beats a persistence baseline on either formal target;
a multi-frequency reduced model misses the 2 % key-region criterion by 27.76 % and 32.19 % on its two
families (13.9× and 16.1×); and the physics gate
passes only under two calibers signed by the reviewer, one of which retires rather than resolves an
electrical-to-acoustic deficit that survives refinement (≈ −3.8 % extrapolated). The closed loop pays off instead: under a
pre-registered fixed budget it reaches zero simple regret after five evaluations, with 96 % of
un-gated proposals outside the supported domain. The corpus, the frozen split and the reference implementation are released as a self-contained
package; the flow is a screening and scheduling layer, not a solver replacement.

**Index Terms**—PMUT, design-space exploration, surrogate modelling, abstention, first resonance
peak, multi-physics simulation.

## 1. Introduction

A PMUT converts electrical drive into out-of-plane membrane motion and thence into an acoustic
pressure field. Its resonance frequency, displacement, pressure sensitivity, and bandwidth depend
jointly on diaphragm geometry, electrode coverage, layer stack, and array layout. Prior work uses
strain mode shapes to guide electrode design and validates the resulting configurations with full
electro-acoustic finite-element (FE) models [1]; others improve displacement or bandwidth through
mass loading and electrode-coverage studies [2], [3]. The physics is well understood, but sweeping continuous and discrete parameters with a full
multi-physics model remains expensive. The evaluation questions this raises — which labels are
comparable, which splits leak, whether a model beats a strong baseline, when it should refuse —
recur wherever a resonant design space is swept with a full-wave solver, not only in PMUTs.

Surrogate models and learning-based optimisation are standard in analog design automation: DNN-Opt
reduces the samples needed for circuit sizing [4]; knowledge-infused learning embeds topology and
performance coupling [5]; multi-agent decomposition shrinks the search space [6]; probabilistic
rollouts cut the number of real simulations [7]; recent frameworks interleave several algorithms to
obtain superadditive gains [8]. Two-stage surrogates [9] and comparative studies [10] likewise target sample efficiency. The shared
objective is not a small average error but finding high-quality designs under a fixed budget.

Applying the same recipe to PMUTs exposes three problems that average error hides.

**P1 — Resonance identity.** A frequency response may contain several local peaks, and the global
maximum of one design need not belong to the same mode order as that of another. Treating "the peak"
as a scalar value silently mixes labels of different physical origin.

**P2 — Incomplete and censored labels.** When the first peak approaches the scan boundary, one or
both −3 dB crossings may be missing, so the bandwidth is not a complete label. If the peak itself
sits on an endpoint, the peak frequency is only a lower bound (right-censored). Such combinations
must not enter a ranking as ordinary measurements.

**P3 — Mixed design space and unsupported extrapolation.** Continuous geometry and integer array
parameters form a mixed space. A plain regressor still returns a number outside its training region,
without saying whether any evidence supports it.

This paper contributes a **benchmark** rather than a new predictor:

1. **B1 — a frozen corpus and a semantically consistent first-peak labelling protocol.** The corpus is
   two scan grids (2,005 rows) plus a frozen 250-row batch. Complete peak, no interior peak,
   unclosed bandwidth, right-censored peak, truncated upper crossing, violated structural
   constraints, and out-of-domain inputs are represented as distinct states, with an explicit
   state-to-field mapping.
2. **B2 — a layered reference implementation for mixed continuous–discrete inputs, with fail-closed
   abstention**
   driven by the input domain, the integer structural constraint, an extrapolation-step limit, and
   quarantined combinations.
3. **B3 — a task specification and an evaluation protocol that names what fails.** Seven tasks, from
   peak identity to closed-loop scheduling, are specified in §S12. We audit the bandwidth caliber, check
   cross-batch consistency, and compare eight methods against persistence under duplicate-aware grouped
   splits with five seeds, reporting the channels that gain nothing instead of a pooled score. The corpus,
   the frozen split, the thresholds and the evaluation code are released as a self-contained package.
4. **B4 — physics-consistency gates reported as experimental results, including the negative ones**:
   limit consistency (S0) passes under a protocol pre-registered before its re-computation while the
   spatial-isotropy caliber registered first still fails; S3 passes **only** after two signed caliber
   rulings, not because the power budget balances; S4 passes only under its declared scope and object;
   the strict mesh-convergence caliber is not met; the solver state stays `draft`.

The evidence supports trend screening and scheduling of high-fidelity re-checks; it does not support
replacing the FE solver or quoting any engineering absolute value.

## 2. Related Work

**PMUT design and modelling.** Mode-shape-guided electrode design [1], mass-loaded arrays [2], and
radial electrode-coverage studies [3] all rely on full FE simulation per candidate. This is precisely
the cost that motivates surrogates, and also the reason a surrogate's validity domain must be stated:
the underlying response is resonant and therefore strongly non-smooth in the design parameters.

**Surrogates and learning-based sizing.** DNN-Opt [4], knowledge infusion [5], multi-agent
reinforcement learning [6], probabilistic rollouts [7], and interworking frameworks [8] reduce
simulation counts for analog circuits, where the label set is well defined. Two-stage surrogate
modelling [9] and surrogate comparisons for signal integrity [10] address accuracy and cost but
assume complete labels. Our focus is the complementary failure mode: labels that exist numerically
yet are not comparable, and predictions that should be refused rather than returned.

**Validation of surrogates.** In this work the surrogate is not only scored by error; it is placed
behind the same kind of consistency gates used for solvers: limit consistency, mesh behaviour,
conservation, and coverage of calibrated intervals. We report gate outcomes verbatim, including
failures, because a validity-aware flow is only useful if its refusals and its negative results are
visible.

## 3. Problem Definition and Label Semantics

**Design variables.** Geometry is varied by a continuous scale factor *x* applied to the membrane
layout, together with two integer array parameters *N₁* (drive-electrode winding count) and *N₂*
(sense-electrode winding count), subject to the structural constraint *N₁* < *N₂*. Frequency is a
separate axis; the present corpus is measured on a fixed frequency grid per batch.

**Response channels.** Eight channels are stored per combination: `bandwidth_Hz`, `Q_factor`,
`f_peak_Hz`, `peak_SPL_dB`, `abs_w_m` (displacement), `abs_p_Pa` and `abs_p_per_V_Pa` (pressure and
pressure per volt), and `abs_vz_struct_m3s` (structural velocity). The source table reports that in the
table `abs_p_Pa` and `abs_p_per_V_Pa` are **byte-identical in all 2005 rows**, i.e. the nominal
eight channels contain **seven independent** ones. All statistics in this paper are therefore stated
per channel *and* per batch, never pooled, and the duplicate pair is flagged wherever channel counts
appear.

**States.** The first observable interior local maximum inside the scan window defines the
first-order resonance peak. The label states are: (i) **complete peak**, both −3 dB crossings inside
the window; (ii) **no interior peak**; (iii) **unclosed bandwidth** (one crossing missing);
(iv) **right-censored peak** (the maximum lies on the window endpoint, so the reported peak frequency
is a lower bound); (v) **truncated upper crossing**; (vi) **structurally invalid** (*N₁* ≥ *N₂*);
(vii) **out of training domain**. A channel value is *defined* for fitting only when it exists, is positive, and its state condition
holds; ratios always report that count, never a flag-based denominator.

**Goal.** Given a corpus of simulated combinations, learn a mapping from (*x*, *N₁*, *N₂*) to the
response channels, quantify its uncertainty, refuse predictions without evidence, and use the result
to allocate a fixed budget of high-fidelity simulations.

## 4. The benchmark and its reference implementation

**4.1 Semantic labelling (B1).** The scan spectrum of each combination is reduced to the state
description of §3 plus the scalar channels, using the exported spectrum and, where available, the
exported peak/bandwidth table. The mapping from each state to the stored fields is tabulated so that
every row can be re-derived and audited; rows whose bandwidth is unclosed or whose peak is censored
are excluded from bandwidth/Q targets rather than imputed.

**4.2 Layered surrogate (B2).** Continuous and discrete inputs are treated separately: within a
fixed integer pair, the continuous axis is modelled with shape-preserving interpolation
(PCHIP) and the integer axis with a per-mode regression, with the two combined in a hierarchical
scheme. Competing global models (linear, kernel ridge, quadratic ridge, Gaussian process, gradient
boosting, MLP) are evaluated as alternatives rather than assumed inferior.

**4.3 Abstention.** A prediction is refused when any of the following holds: the query leaves the
declared applicability box; the integer constraint *N₁* < *N₂* is violated; the requested
extrapolation exceeds the declared number of steps; the combination is on the quarantine list; or the
requested channel is undefined for that state. Refusals are counted and reported; the flow never
substitutes a value silently, and out-of-domain queries are referred back to the FE solver.

**4.4 Calibrated intervals.** Interval predictions are produced on the calibrated subset and are
evaluated by empirical coverage in the range required by the solver specification, with interval
width reported alongside coverage. A narrow interval with low coverage is reported as a failure, not
as precision.

## 5. Experimental Setup

**Model and mesh.** The FE model is a 3-D piezoelectric–acoustic PMUT stack (seven-layer device on a
handle wafer) solved in the frequency domain with a fixed design point per simulation. All physics-gate numbers below use the pre-registered caliber: a boundary mesh on the solid/electrode
side plus a tetrahedral acoustic mesh of `lamda/5` referenced to `fmax = 35 MHz`, so the mesh does
**not** change with the solved frequency.

**Data.** Two scan grids coexist and are never merged: batch C (5–50 MHz, 91 frequencies,
1,782 rows) and batch A (5–35 MHz, 61 frequencies, 223 rows), plus a 250-row frozen batch V2 kept
separate because its flags are proxies rather than measured states. Of the C rows, 1,523 (85.5 %)
have a closed two-sided −3 dB bandwidth and 673 meet the pre-set quality-factor gate; for A the
corresponding numbers are 154 of 223 (69.1 %) and 55.

**Splits.** All learned models share one external split artifact with five seeds. Its grouping variable
is **duplicate clusters, not geometry families**: near-duplicate rows (541 of 2,255, 24 %) stay on one
side, so contamination through *exactly repeated* design points is structurally zero (each geometry
cell and continuous-coordinate combination occurs once). The split is **not** disjoint along the
continuous axis: at the C batch's step of 0.01, **91 %** of held-out rows have a training neighbour
one grid step away (87 % and 90 % in the two coarser batches, step 0.1). We state this because
"grouped" must name what it groups — this split removes duplicate leakage, not interpolation
leakage — and because a size-matched uniform random split is geometrically equivalent to it
(adjacent-step fraction within 4 points) yet moves the median error of 9 of 63 C-batch cells by more
than 10 % in both directions, so the residual spread is split-realization noise rather than an
effect of the grouping rule (§S11).

**Baselines and metrics.** Eight methods are compared: persistence (copy the nearest available
neighbour in the integer direction within the same continuous layer), PCHIP, linear regression,
kernel ridge, quadratic ridge, Gaussian process, gradient boosting, and an MLP. The primary metric is
the median relative error `|pred − truth| / truth` with the count of defined rows; the paired
comparison metric is `lift_paired` = median error of persistence minus median error of the method on
the same secondary subset, so that a positive value means genuine added value. No weighted total
score is computed.

**Gate semantics.** Physics gates are taken from the solver specification, whose thresholds are
S0 (low-frequency limit consistency < 1 %), S1 (key region < 2 %, global < 5 %), S2 (engineering
quantities < 3 %), S3 (conservation < 0.5 %, boundary conditions < 1 %), S4 (mesh difference < 2 %)
and S5 (interval coverage in [0.85, 0.95]).
An `inconclusive` outcome is never reported as a pass, and thresholds are never adjusted after the
fact.

*Supplementary material (§S1–§S8) carries the per-cell, per-variant and historical
detail; every headline number appears in this body.*

## 6. Results

### 6.1 Label validity is part of the design problem

In batch C, 259 of 1,782 rows (14.5 %) do not have a closed two-sided bandwidth, and of the 1,523
closed rows **673 (44.2 %) meet the quality-factor gate**; in batch A, 69 of 223 rows (30.9 %) are
not closed and 55 of the 154 closed rows (35.7 %) meet the gate. Both counts are taken from
the source table's own columns — `bandwidth_closed` for closure and `q_gate_pass` (interval 2.5–3.5)
for the gate, whose applicability column equals the closed subset exactly — **not** from the
downstream `peak_closed` flag, which counts 1,612 and 161 closed rows respectively and would
therefore give a different denominator. These are not noise: they are the expected consequence of a
first peak that drifts towards or across the scan window as the design moves. Any pipeline that
treats them as ordinary targets silently mixes complete, censored, and truncated labels; §4.1
therefore excludes them from the bandwidth and Q targets and reports the surviving counts.

### 6.2 The bandwidth caliber must be named

Two independent audit comparisons exist, and they must not be merged into one sentence.

* The single-degree-of-freedom fitted bandwidth versus batch A's own `from_points` estimate, on the
  same peak: **median |ΔBW|/BW = 20.3 %**, p90 27.2 %, within-10 % rate 11.0 %, Spearman 0.9888
  (n = 91) — a systematic ≈0.8× scale offset with a **rank-robust** ordering.
* The locally extracted bandwidth versus the exported table over the same combinations:
  **median 33.5 %**, p90 88.1 %, within-10 % rate 21.9 % (n = 292), and the quality factors of the
  two calibers correlate only 0.2226 — i.e. this comparison is **not** rank-robust, and the exported
  table is retained as the primary caliber for Q and bandwidth.

Consequently, absolute bandwidth values are reported with their caliber; cross-caliber comparisons
are restricted to rankings, and only where the rank correlation was verified.

### 6.3 Strong baselines: no method beats persistence on the two targets

Under duplicate-aware grouped splits (§4.1), per-batch training, and five seeds, 20 channel×batch
cells are decidable. For the two formal targets the outcome is negative and consistent across seeds:

| channel (batch C) | best method | median relative error | `lift_paired` (median) | seeds +/ − |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bandwidth_Hz` | gradient boosting | 0.01436 | **−0.005988** | 0 / 5 |
| `Q_factor` | gradient boosting | 0.009553 | **−0.001968** | 0 / 5 |

The persistence baseline itself has median relative error 0.007647 (`bandwidth_Hz`) and 0.007321
(`Q_factor`) on the same batch. Across the full table, only 10 of 20 cells contain any method that is
positive in all five seeds, and in 8 of 20 cells the best method has a negative median lift. Four
channels do gain value (`peak_SPL_dB`, `abs_w_m`, `abs_p_Pa`/`abs_p_per_V_Pa`, `abs_vz_struct_m3s`),
but these are precisely the channels whose persistence baseline is weak because adjacent integer
layers differ substantially. Counting every channel, 8 of the 20 decidable cells admit no method that
beats persistence in the median (three channels in each of A and C, two in V2), and the size-matched
random split of §S11 gives 7: the two rules agree, so the negative result is not an artefact of the
split rule.

An independent implementation that does not import the evaluation code re-derived these numbers: the
manifest (12 files) verifies hash-for-hash, the training/test key sets are disjoint for all five seeds,
and the persistence median 0.007726751204325416 and its defined-row count (416) reproduce exactly. We report the limits of that verification as
well: the full 8-method table, the six ablations, the PCHIP implementation, batch V2, and the
holdout-label-based active-sampling study were **not** independently recomputed by that implementation.
To make them checkable we release the corpus, the frozen split, the thresholds and the evaluation code
as a self-contained benchmark package: one command reproduces every per-seed per-cell median of the
full table plus all six ablations in about seven minutes, with a maximum relative deviation of 0.0
against the frozen reference in our environment (no third party has run it yet).

The conclusion is deliberately narrow: *in this corpus, under these calibers, exchanging the
surrogate for a stronger regressor does not pay off on the two formal targets.* It does not show that
surrogate modelling is useless — it shows that label semantics, not model capacity, is the binding
constraint.

### 6.4 Physics-consistency gates

**S3 passes — but only under two calibers signed by the reviewer.** On the gate design point the
registered S3 report gives 7 passes and 2 failures. Correcting the current-conservation check first
makes the count **8 passes and 1 failure**, with 0 inconclusive outcomes (signed 2026-10-12): that
check compared a lumped circuit current with a field flux across an unclosed circuit using
post-processing normals, and re-evaluated as a closed-surface flux with the physics normal it gives
6.469e-04…1.032e-03 on one carrier and 8.168e-04 on a second, inside the 0.5 % criterion. The
remaining failure was then **re-scoped rather than re-solved** (signed 2026-10-13): the
electrical-to-acoustic caliber under which it failed — the input power is 6.177 % below the radiated
acoustic power, and §6.5 shows that this survives refinement — is retired as the gate's criterion
while its reading is kept, and the operative criterion becomes the acoustic-side net outward flux of
§6.5, which measures **0.035–0.383 %** across 5–35 MHz. With both rulings all nine registered checks
pass. **The pass therefore records two ruled calibers, not a balanced power budget**: the
electrical-to-acoustic deficit stands at 6.177 % and extrapolates to ≈ −3.8 %, about eight times the
criterion, so any claim that the model's input and radiated powers agree would contradict §6.5.

**S0 passes under a pre-registered protocol, and one registered caliber still fails.** The protocol
was frozen before its zero-solve re-computation. On the solver's own material constants the
low-frequency identity ρ_eff(f) = ∫|p|²dA / (2c·P_rad) must equal 998.2024166 kg/m³ exactly for a
monopole; at 0.25 MHz it gives a relative error of **9.0380e-04**, 11.1× inside the 1 % criterion,
and reproduces the recorded values bit-for-bit (**1.5230e-02** at 20 MHz is reported as well, that
point having left the monopole asymptotics). The caliber registered *first* — spatial isotropy and an
r⁻¹ law — **still fails** (0.0090833 and 0.0290035 against 0.01) and is reported alongside it. The
residual does not fall monotonically to zero, so a systematic floor of ≈0.03–0.09 % remains; only the
monopole component is covered; and the carrier is a λ/5 mesh whose binding to the solver instances is
untested. The pre-registration covers the *evaluation protocol* only: the choice of ρ_eff itself was
made after the isotropy caliber failed and is therefore still post-hoc.

**S1/S2/S5 at the local level, and S4 under a declared scope.** On five held-out continuous values at
20 MHz a shape-preserving local model meets all three thresholds — key region **0.69999 %** (whole
domain 1.09097 %) for *N₁* = 4 and **0.95735 %** (1.13266 %) for *N₁* = 7, with an engineering maximum
of **0.739 %** — while a raw-POD variant fails the key region at 2.126 %; the coverage protocol
promoted by the human reviewer on 2026-10-10 yields 0.9259 / 0.9383. On the main mesh-sensitivity axis
(nine levels, 72 ordered pairs) the key region gives **0.6753 % < 2 %** and the whole-domain reference
1.5504 %; Richardson extrapolations under three caliper choices give 1.3446 %, 1.4300 % and 1.4963 %.
S4 therefore passes **only** on the levels tested, under a caliber **frozen before the 2026-10-12 re-analysis**, and must not be read as
mesh independence. Strict convergence remains unestablished: the recorded strict check fails at
2.235 %, the finest-pair caliber the reviewer later approved passes, and both readings are reported. The 2026-10-12 re-analysis froze the caliber and every byte anchor *before* running and reproduced all twelve recorded values **bit-for-bit**; it nevertheless re-uses the **same solved fields**, so it is a procedural fix and **not** an independent confirmation, and the *choice* of spatial object is inherited from the earlier post-hoc ruling rather than cleansed by it. A **fresh re-solve** of the same ladder (same model, same mesh) returns the same verdict but is **not bit-identical**: the twelve values differ by ~1e-13, so the solver is deterministic only to ~1e-12. Node coordinates and export sizes are bit-identical, which excludes storage and decoding error; the re-solve is still **not** an independent confirmation.
No engineering absolute value is quoted anywhere in this paper.

**Field-level thresholds stay closed.** After correcting an object mismatch the field-level gate is
**0/7** folds at the key-region 2 % object, **0/7** at the 3 % engineering object and **2/7** at the
global 5 % object; two models have bit-identical maxima but different fold-pass counts and the
authoritative artifact names the less flattering one. A group of 405 combinations stays unexpanded.
Per-check tables, the ladder history and the full caveat list are in §S2–§S5; the field-level representation study is in §S10.

### 6.5 Diagnosing the energy-closure residual

We probed S3's energy item directly, entirely by zero-solve post-processing on already-solved models
(full seven-row probe table in §S6).

| candidate cause | measurement | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| port-voltage convention | drive −1.000000 V, ground 0, floating terminal ≤ 7.7 µV; including it changes `P_in` by +0.053 % | refuted |
| current post-expression | `∫es.JZ dA ≡ jω·∫es.Dz dA` on all four electrode sets, to all printed digits | refuted |
| plane-wave acoustic power | the model's `Ptot` equals the exact flux `Im(K)/(2ωρ)` at all six frequencies | refuted |
| integration surface | the two patches are the **complementary halves of the one provably closed boundary** `∂(domain 1)` — an exhaustive search over all 65,535 domain subsets finds exactly one closed set — and their orientation is **measured per face**, not assumed; their flux difference is a genuine net outward flux (0.077 % at 5 MHz rising to 4.49 % at 35 MHz), not a surface disagreement | refuted |
| fixed systematic offset | a second geometry at the same 20 MHz gives +1.60 % / −0.34 % instead of −4.12 % / −5.82 %; within one geometry the sign flips across 5–35 MHz | refuted |
| unconverged discretisation | solid-side ×1 → ×0.75 → ×0.5: −4.852 % → −4.384 % → **−4.121 %** (decrement ratio 0.562 ⇒ limit ≈ **−3.8 %**); acoustic mesh changes it by 0.0011 pp | refuted |

The calibrated statement is therefore: **at this design point** the electrical input power is about
4.4 % below the acoustic flux, this is a genuine discrete non-closure rather than a post-processing
mistake, and it **does not vanish with refinement** — the extrapolated limit, ≈ −3.8 %, is roughly
eight times the 0.5 % criterion. The same caliber on a second, independently meshed carrier reproduces
the shortfall (−5.82 % and −4.12 % versus −6.57 % and −4.85 %) with the three powers agreeing between
carriers to within 0.81 %; that agreement is itself informative, since the second carrier's powers match
the ×0.5 solid-side refinement to 1.1e-10 relative. The residual is design-point- and
frequency-dependent (it changes sign), so a single figure such as "≈4.4 %" is valid for the reported
design point only and must never be presented as a property of the method. Three calibers travel with
every such statement: `P_in`-based energy statements are meaningful only above ≈2 MHz; post-processing
normals must not be used for closed-surface flux judges (they give ≈0.999 where the physics normal gives
8.2e-04); and because the sign depends on the integration surface at some frequencies, the surface
caliber must be named each time.

**Two calibers must be kept apart.** The table above is the *electrical-to-acoustic* caliber, whose non-vanishing limit (≈ −3.8 %) reflects an intentionally incomplete circuit model. On the *acoustic side alone* a circuit-free criterion exists — the net outward flux over the closed fluid boundary must vanish — and it behaves differently: **0.0769 %** at 5 MHz and **0.4660 %** at 10 MHz, but **1.07–4.49 %** from 15 to 35 MHz, converging with refinement as **1.7706 % → 0.9540 % → 0.5632 %** at 20 MHz (λ/5 → λ/7 → λ/9), a three-point fit giving **p = 1.94 (R² = 0.999)**. It is therefore a **discretisation error of the acoustic mesh**, not a missing dissipation path (§S9). Even λ/9 remains above the 0.5 % criterion (0.5632 %), but λ/11 (862,410 elements) brings 20 MHz to **0.3862 %**, inside it. Extending the same treatment across the band with a per-frequency mesh measures **0.035, 0.137, 0.207, 0.365, 0.377, 0.354 and 0.383 %** at 5–35 MHz — **all seven inside the inherited τ = 0.5 %**, the upper band by only ≈1.3×. This is a single-axis (`ftet1`) result on one carrier and one design point, not mesh independence; two operational deviations and two infrastructure aborts in the finest attempt travel with it (§S9). On 2026-10-13 the reviewer adopted this acoustic-side caliber as the operative energy criterion of S3, retiring — without erasing — the electrical-to-acoustic reading of the table above; the two calibers must therefore always be named, because only one of them is now the gate.

### 6.6 Closed-loop design-space exploration

**Closed loop (pre-registered, completed).** Four arms — random, greedy, Gaussian-process
expected improvement, and the same with an applicability gate — shared a pool of 1080 candidate designs
and five initial points under a fixed budget of 105 single-frequency evaluations of radiated acoustic
power per volt² at 20 MHz. The objective spans 574× across the 93 designs evaluated, the campaign
finished in 123 minutes with a 2 % failure rate, and the pre-registered primary endpoint is decisive:

| arm | calls | best objective (W/V²) | simple regret | best at k = 5 | top-10 % hit rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| random (5 seeds × 5) | 25 | 2.1756e-05 | 18.8 % | 8.409e-06 (median) | 0.0 % |
| greedy | 25 | 2.2807e-05 | 14.9 % | 4.863e-06 | 4.0 % |
| GP + expected improvement | 25 | **2.6786e-05** | **0.00 %** | **2.640e-05** | **24.0 %** |
| GP + EI, gated | 23 | 2.6126e-05 | 2.46 % | 2.510e-05 | 17.4 % |

After five evaluations the GP arms exceed every one of the five random seeds (quantile 1.00) and both
beat the random arm's best value over its entire 25-call budget. A post-hoc replay shows that **24 of
the 25 ungated proposals would have landed outside the corpus-covered domain** (96 %), so abstention is
load-bearing rather than cosmetic. The claim is bounded: the oracle is single-frequency, so this is a
protocol-efficiency comparison and not a bandwidth result, and only the random arm has seed replication.

**Multi-frequency ROM (pre-registered, completed).** The third experiment asks whether single-frequency
evidence extends along the frequency axis; it fails, and the failure is diagnosed. The registered grid
placed eleven of thirteen frequencies inside 11.1–12.9 MHz and **none** between 6.3–11.1 or
12.9–18.0 MHz, while a coefficient-shape diagnosis showed the resonance phase swinging 2.8 rad across
the first of those empty intervals. On the nine cross cells per family the registered ROM's key-region
error is **27.76 %** (*N₁* = 4) and **32.19 %** (*N₁* = 7) against 2 %, with whole-domain values
27.77 % / 31.68 % against 5 %.

*Disclosure.* The first run of this analysis covered **3 of those 9 cells**: the registration stored the
held-out frequencies as `[11.399999999999999, 12, 12.600000000000001]` while the field dictionary is
keyed by exact values, and an exact-equality cell filter silently dropped the columns `11.4` and
`12.6`; the first-reported figures (27.1 % / 30.9 %) were therefore computed on `f = 12.0` alone. The
defect is in the analysis script, not in the data or the model, and it is disclosed because the earlier
numbers appear in this paper's revision history; §S1 gives the corrected per-variant values and the
affected artifacts are retained unchanged.

Raising the basis order from the registered k = 4 to the full rank k = 120 drives the training
reconstruction error to 0.000 % while the cross-cell error stays at **27.64 % / 31.79 %**, so the
reduced basis is *not* the limiting factor — the corrected numbers make that conclusion stronger.
A pre-registered re-design then changed **only** the frequency axis (a quarter-octave grid covering both
flanks, with the three held-out frequencies placed **on the flanks**; 360 further solves, zero failures,
102 minutes). No reconstruction reaches the criterion: the best is `cubic` at **26.46 %** / 44.84 %,
and the single-pole resonance parameterisation fails **on its own training data** (training control
68.56 % / 76.13 %, per-mode fit residual 27.22 % / 23.98 %). Two things follow. First, frequency
sampling was *not* the binding constraint, so densifying the axis the diagnosis identified does not
rescue this ROM. Second, a single-pole Lorentzian plus a complex baseline cannot represent these
coefficient curves at all. The pre-registered fallback therefore applies: change the *representation*
rather than the threshold — a multi-pole or complex-pole (vector-fitting) form, an explicit per-mode
propagation phase, or an operator-learning surrogate — and S5 is deliberately left unevaluated because
its (*x*, *f*) protocol has not been pre-registered.

## 7. Discussion

**Semantics before capacity.** The negative baseline result of §6.3 and the label analysis of §6.1
point the same way: in this problem the binding constraint is whether a number is a comparable
measurement, not how expressive the regressor is. Spending capacity on a target whose label is
censored in 15–31 % of rows buys nothing.

**Abstention is a feature with a price.** Refusing out-of-domain queries prevents silent extrapolation
but removes coverage: disabling abstention raises coverage to 1 while the fallback-filled subset often
has *lower* median error, so "abstention blocks the hard cases, therefore it is more accurate" does not
follow. Any gate must be reported with the coverage it costs.

**Gates are results.** Three of the four physics checks here are negative or narrowly scoped.
Reporting them as results rather than as diagnostics is what makes the refusal mechanism auditable —
and what forbids the engineering claims a smaller-error surrogate would tempt one to make.

## 8. Conclusion

PeakGuard couples resonance-semantic labelling, a layered mixed-input surrogate, calibrated intervals
and fail-closed abstention. On this corpus the honest summary is: label validity and gate status, not
prediction accuracy, dominate usefulness; no tested method beats persistence on the two formal targets;
and the physics gate passes only under two calibers signed by the reviewer, one of which retires
rather than resolves an electrical-to-acoustic deficit that survives refinement. The flow is
therefore offered as a candidate screener and high-fidelity scheduling layer. Experimental anchoring, a validated field-level solver, and a frequency-axis reduced model that meets
the key-region criterion remain future work; the closed loop and the first multi-frequency protocol are
reported here, the latter as a diagnosed failure.

**Limitations.** (i) No experimental data and no literature evidence rows are in the repository;
all statements are simulation-internal. (ii) Strict mesh convergence is not established, so no
engineering absolute value may be quoted. (iii) S0 and S3 — the two first-hop conditions — are now
met, yet **no state has been promoted**: promotion is a human act and no engineering absolute value
follows from it. The model's own status remains `draft`. (iv) A large re-export of a second scan grid (648 combinations × 91 frequencies) is
available, but its overlap regression against the earlier grid fails (maximum |Δ| = 0.08849 dB over
4,392 overlapping points) because the two exports differ in source model and scan definition; it is
reported as a limitation, not as a pipeline failure. (v) Both pre-registered experiments of §6.6 are complete; their
narrow or negative outcomes are reported as results, not withheld. (vi) The energy-closure residual of
§6.5 is a *per-point* quantity that changes sign with frequency and design point; the value quoted for
the reported design point is not a global constant, and the supporting measurements were obtained by
zero-solve post-processing of already-solved models rather than by new solves. It also does **not**
vanish under mesh refinement: on the reporting design point it falls from −4.85 % to −4.12 % as the
solid-side mesh is halved twice, and extrapolates to ≈ −3.8 %, about eight times the 0.5 % criterion.
An engine-level caliber or model change — not more resolution — would be required to close it, and the
ruling of 2026-10-13 is precisely such a caliber change: it retires the reading rather than removing
the deficit.
(vii) An analysis-script defect is disclosed above: the first multi-frequency cross-cell figures covered
3 of the 9 cells because a floating-point frequency key was matched by exact equality. The corrected
values are reported in this revision; the defect changed no measured quantity and no verdict.
